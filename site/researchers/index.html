Why the letter takes so long

By the time the reviews come back, the science is done, and the response letter turns into a project of its own. You have to match the reviewers' numbering, get the tone right and decide how firmly to push back on comment 3 from Reviewer 2.

The decisions are the hard part, and they're yours. After that the letter follows a known format, and turning your decisions into a numbered point-by-point letter is work a model is good at.

What the skill does

  1. Paste the reviews, your paper summary and your decisions The reviewer reports, labelled by reviewer, and the paper title and abstract. For each comment, say whether you accept it, partly accept it or push back, and what you changed.
  2. Each comment gets an ID, a class and a draft response The class is Accept (A), Partial (B) or Pushback (C), taken from your decisions. Each response says what you changed and where. The skill is instructed to use only the details you give it and to put the rest in brackets, including any decision you haven't made. Check every specific before you send.
  3. You get the whole letter An opening paragraph, every response in order and a summary of the major changes, ready to paste into the journal's submission system.
Illustrative example for an urban tree canopy paper in Urban Climate. [Brackets] mark details you supply.

Comment R1.1: "The methods section does not explain how 'matched controls' were selected. This is a critical omission."

(A) Accept Response: We thank the reviewer for identifying this gap. We have added a paragraph to [section] describing how matched controls were selected: [your matching criteria and method].

Comment R2.1: "The conclusion section reads as if causation is established."

(A) Accept Response: We agree that our design shows association, not causation. We have revised the conclusion ([where]) to replace causal language with associational language, and added [the caveat you wrote about causal inference].

What it does with awkward comments

If a reviewer's comment contradicts itself, the skill points out the contradiction and asks how you want to handle it before drafting, instead of guessing.

If a reviewer wants you to change a conclusion you stand by, it drafts a firm but collegial pushback from the justification you give it.

And if you forget to paste the reviews, it asks for them. It won't make them up.
