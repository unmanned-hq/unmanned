Why the fit check comes first

A full proposal takes days, and too much of that time goes to opportunities that were never a fit. Maybe the funder wants rural health programmes and your organisation is urban, or it expects a direct-service track record and yours is in capacity building. Often you only learn that at the debrief, six months later.

Checking fit takes one sitting. I think it belongs before any drafting, and it's the first thing to go when a deadline is close.

What the skill does

  1. Paste the RFP and your org profile The whole RFP, or at least its eligibility, priorities and award range. For the profile, a paragraph on your mission, geography, budget and recent work is enough.
  2. It grades three things Mission alignment, eligibility and capacity each get Strong, Partial or Weak, with a one-line reason. It also quotes the funder's priorities back to you in the RFP's own words.
  3. The verdict comes with next steps For a High, it lists the three requirements most likely to be thin in your first draft. For a Medium, it suggests two or three actions that could close the gap before you submit. For a Low, it names the blocker plainly so you can move on.
