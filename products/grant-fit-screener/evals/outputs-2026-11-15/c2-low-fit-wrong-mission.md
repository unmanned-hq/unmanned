# Grant Fit Memo: Bright Futures Education Fund

## Funder priorities (their words)

- "K-12 schools and education nonprofits in Texas"
- "STEM enrichment programmes"
- "serving low-income students"
- "Applicants must be Texas-based"
- "demonstrate measurable student learning outcomes from prior programmes"

## Dimension scores

| Dimension | Score | One-line reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission alignment | Weak | The funder wants STEM enrichment for students. Your work is community health clinics and mobile vaccination, and you have no education programming. |
| Eligibility | Weak | The fund requires applicants to be Texas-based K-12 schools or education nonprofits. You are a health equity nonprofit based in Chicago, Illinois. |
| Capacity | Weak | Applicants must show student learning outcomes from prior programmes. With no education programming, you have no such outcomes to show. |

## Verdict

**Low.** You fail two hard eligibility criteria (Texas-based, education organisation) and cannot meet the prior-outcomes requirement, so don't apply.

## Blockers

1. **Geography.** The RFP says "Applicants must be Texas-based." You are based in Chicago, Illinois. This is a hard criterion, and no amount of proposal writing can change it.
2. **Organisation type.** Awards go only to "K-12 schools and education nonprofits." You are a health equity nonprofit.
3. **No prior outcomes.** Applicants must "demonstrate measurable student learning outcomes from prior programmes." You have no education programming, so you have no outcomes to report.
4. **Mission mismatch.** STEM enrichment for low-income students is unrelated to clinics and vaccination programmes. You would have to invent a programme outside your mission just to apply.

Partnering or changing how you frame the proposal won't clear blockers 1 and 2, because the fund screens on who the applicant is. Spend the hours on funders whose priorities include health equity, community health or vaccination access in Illinois or Chicago.
