## Sharpened topic

**Started with:** "Why I stopped checking email in the morning and what I do instead."

**Arrived at:** If you check email first thing, the start of your day goes to other people's priorities. Spending that time on [what you do instead] is a better use of it.

## Hook/headline options

- **Direct:** Checking email first thing gives your morning away. Here's what I do with mine now: [what you do instead].
- **Curiosity:** I used to open my inbox before anything else. Then I tried [what you do instead], and [what changed for you].
- **Contrarian:** "Inbox zero before 9" sounds productive. For me it was [the cost you noticed].

## Issue structure

| Section | Description | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The habit I dropped | What your morning email routine looked like, what it cost you ([the cost you noticed]), and what finally made you stop ([your trigger]). | 150–200 |
| 2. What I do instead | Your replacement routine, step by step: [what you do instead], [when you finally open email], and [how you handle anything urgent]. | 250–350 |
| 3. How to try it yourself | A small version readers can test tomorrow, the obstacle they'll most likely hit ([the hardest part for you]), and what changed for you ([your results]). | 150–200 |

Total: about 550–750 words.
