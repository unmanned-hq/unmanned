Happy to help. One thing first: "AI" on its own covers too much for one issue. It could be ten different issues (tools, jobs, ethics, regulation, hype, your own workflow, and more), and a plan built on it would come out generic. Let's narrow it to one specific claim before I build the plan.

Here are three narrower versions. Pick one, or tell me your own angle:

1. **AI in your own work.** One specific way you use (or stopped using) an AI tool, and what it changed. *Example claim: "[AI tool] saves me [time] on [task], but costs me [trade-off]."* You'd fill in the details from your own experience. I won't guess them.

2. **AI for your readers' field.** What AI actually changes, and doesn't change, for the people who read your newsletter. *Example claim: "For [your readers' role], AI is most useful for [task X] and overrated for [task Y]."*

3. **A take on the AI conversation.** Pushback on one popular belief about AI that you think is wrong or overstated. *Example claim: "The common advice to [popular AI belief] is [wrong / incomplete] because [your reason]."*

Two optional things that will sharpen the plan:
- **Who reads your newsletter?** One sentence is enough. If you skip this, I'll state an assumption and go ahead.
- **Roughly how many subscribers do you have?** This decides the call to action: under 500, I'll ask readers for a share or a reply; 500 or more, I can ask for a purchase, a hire or a referral. If you don't say, I'll default to "reply with your take."

Once you've picked a direction, I'll send the full one-page plan: sharpened topic, 3 hook options, a 3-section structure with word counts, a CTA, and 3 subject lines.
