I split Reviewer 1's comment into three points: the measurement limitation (R1.1), option (a) device-log validation (R1.2), and option (b) reframing as a pilot (R1.3). The reviewer offers (a) and (b) as alternatives, so you may answer them differently, for example partly accepting one and pushing back on the other.

**Two things to settle before you fill this in:**

1. **The reviewer's reasoning about effect sizes isn't clear, so I haven't guessed at it.** The reviewer says parental report underestimates exposure by 30-40% and concludes that your effect sizes "are likely underestimates." Whether that follows depends on what kind of error it is. If every family under-reports by a similar proportion, the estimated effect per hour of screen time could be *overstated* rather than understated. It also changes who falls above your >3 hours/day cut-off. Random, non-differential error would usually weaken (attenuate) the association, which fits the reviewer's view. Error that differs by group (for example by SES or by EF outcome) could push it either way. How do you want to handle this? You could (i) accept the reviewer's framing, (ii) push back on the direction of bias, or (iii) say the direction is uncertain and report a sensitivity analysis. I've added an optional pushback sentence to R1.1 and R1.3. Please check it against your analysis before using it. The reviewer also gives no source for the 30-40% figure, so I haven't cited one.
2. **Reframing as a pilot study (option b) would change what the paper claims.** Making that change only to satisfy the reviewer isn't something the letter should do on its own. If you take this route, it's a deliberate decision about the paper's scientific framing, not a wording edit.
