**Reviewer 1**

**Comment R1.1:** "The heterogeneity across studies (I²=78%) is too high to pool results in a meta-analysis. The authors should use a narrative synthesis instead."

**Classification:** Pushback

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for raising the issue of between-study heterogeneity, which is an important consideration when interpreting any pooled estimate. We agree that an I² of 78% indicates substantial heterogeneity. However, we respectfully disagree that it rules out quantitative pooling. Substantial heterogeneity is common in research on the gut microbiome and depression [supporting reference(s)]. It reflects expected variation in populations, sequencing methods and diversity metrics, not a flaw specific to this dataset. We pooled the 34 studies (n=6,200) using a random-effects model. That model does not assume a single true effect. It is designed for this situation: it estimates the mean of a distribution of true effects and widens the confidence interval to account for between-study variance [supporting methodological reference(s)]. A narrative synthesis would not remove this heterogeneity. It would only make it harder to quantify, and it would give up the formal estimate of effect size and precision (SMD = −0.41, 95% CI −0.55 to −0.27) that a random-effects meta-analysis provides.
